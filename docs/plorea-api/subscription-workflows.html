Recurring billing

Subscription workflows

Step-by-step recipes for the changes that happen during a subscription's life: a free trial, a price increase, an upgrade, a pause, a new card, a cancellation. Each recipe says when to use it, which calls to make, and what can go wrong.

Read this first

The Plorea API has a small set of subscription operations: create, read, update (PATCH), charge now, cancel and reactivate. PATCH also pauses and resumes. The API has no proration and no interval change. The recipes below build those workflows from the operations that exist. Where a recipe depends on behaviour we could not verify, the badge says so.

The building blocks

Every recipe on this page combines the same six operations. Learn their effects once.

OperationEffect on billingEvidence
POST /subscriptionsStarts billing immediately. With trialEndsAt, the first charge happens at the end of the trial.Verified
PATCH /subscriptions/{id}Changes the amount, VAT, quantity, labels or card. Moves nextChargeAt. Pauses with status: "paused" and resumes with status: "active". It does not charge. It cannot change the interval.Verified in test
POST /subscriptions/{id}/chargeCharges the stored card now. It does not move the schedule of later charges in a way you control.Verified
POST /subscriptions/{id}/cancelStops future charges. Returns accessEndsAt = last charge + one interval.Verified
POST /subscriptions/{id}/reactivateRestarts a canceled subscription. Sets nextChargeAt to last charge + one interval. Does not charge twice.Verified
GET /subscriptions/{id}/chargesLists the charges, newest first. The only reliable record of scheduled billing.Verified

Three rules apply to every recipe:

  • Keep your own copy of the plan. Store the subscription ID, the amount, the interval and the company it bills for in your database. Reads return merchantOrgNr, but not merchantName or merchantEmail. Verified
  • Gate access on dates, not on status. Give access while status is active or trialing, or while accessEndsAt is in the future. A past_due subscription is in its retry window. Your grace policy decides its access. See Failed charges and dunning.
  • Do not retry a charge or a create blindly. The API has no idempotency keys. A retried call can charge twice or create two subscriptions. See Errors and retries. Verified

Sign-up with a free trial

Use this when the customer gets a free period before the first charge. The same recipe also gives you a fixed billing date, for example "always bill on the 1st": set the trial to end on that date.

A trial needs a stored card first. The card setup makes a 1 NOK verification authorisation, so the customer proves the card works before the trial starts. Verified

  1. Store the cardCall POST /payment-methods/setup with recurringType: "Subscription". Send the customer to adyenPaymentLinkUrl. See Storing cards.
  2. Wait for the card to become activePoll GET /payment-methods/{id} after the customer returns. active means you can subscribe. failed is final: start a new setup. No webhook is sent for setup. Stated by Plorea
  3. Create the subscription with a trial endSend trialEndsAt in ISO 8601. The response has status: "trialing" and nextChargeAt equal to trialEndsAt. Verified
  4. Save the subscription IDStore subscriptionId against your customer record before you do anything else.
  5. Give access during the trialTreat trialing as an access-granting status. It is a separate status, not a kind of active. Verified
  6. Extend access on the first chargeWhen subscription.charge_succeeded arrives, read the subscription and extend access to the new nextChargeAt.

Pitfalls

  • Without trialEndsAt, billing starts at once. The scheduler charges within seconds of create. There is no "start on date X" option except a trial. Verified
  • A cancel during the trial returns accessEndsAt: null. The value is derived from the last charge, and a trial has none. Treat null as "access ends now". Do not treat it as "access never ends". Verified
  • A trialEndsAt in the past has unknown behaviour. Always send a future date. Open question
  • Send vatAmount as the VAT part of amount. All recorded responses fit VAT-inclusive amounts: for 25 % VAT, vatAmount = amount × 0.25 / 1.25, so 19900 øre gives 3980 øre. Plorea has not documented this. Open question

Raise the price

Use this when you change the price of a plan for existing subscribers, for example a yearly price adjustment. The customer keeps the same card and the same billing date. The next renewal uses the new price.

  1. Tell the customerConsumer rules and card scheme rules usually require advance notice of a price change. Check which rules apply to you. Send the notice before the renewal that uses the new price. State the new amount and the date of the first charge at that amount.
  2. Read the subscriptionCall GET /subscriptions/{id}. Note nextChargeAt. Confirm that the subscription is active or trialing.
  3. Send the new amount and the new VAT togetherCall PATCH /subscriptions/{id} with amount, vatRate and vatAmount. Send the VAT fields in the same call. Otherwise the stored VAT no longer matches the amount.
  4. Time the PATCH between two renewalsThe new amount applies to the next renewal the scheduler runs. Plorea has no effective-date field. PATCH after the last renewal at the old price has completed. Do not PATCH in the last hour before the first renewal at the new price. The scheduler may already be processing the charge.
  5. Check the next chargeAfter the renewal, read GET /subscriptions/{id}/charges. Confirm that the newest charge has the new amount.

What we know

  • The PATCH stores the new amount and returns it. It does not charge and does not move nextChargeAt. Verified
  • The next scheduled charge uses the stored amount at the time it runs. This is the expected behaviour, but no renewal after a PATCH has been observed yet. Check the first renewal in production. Open question
  • There is no "effective from" date on a PATCH. You control timing only by when you send it.

Grandfathering

To keep old customers on the old price, do nothing to their subscriptions. Price lives on each subscription, not on a shared plan. New sign-ups simply get the new amount at create.

Upgrade mid-cycle

Use this when a customer moves to a more expensive plan and should get it now, not at the next renewal. Plorea has no proration. You calculate the difference for the rest of the period and charge it once. Then you PATCH the subscription so that later renewals use the new price.

Work out the difference

Use the remaining share of the current period. The formula below rounds to whole øre. Use the same rounding rule in your invoices.

remaining_ratio = (nextChargeAt − now) / (nextChargeAt − periodStart)
difference      = round((newAmount − oldAmount) × remaining_ratio)
vatAmount       = round(difference × vatRate / (1 + vatRate))

Example: 199 kr → 399 kr, 15 of 30 days left
difference = (39900 − 19900) × 15/30 = 10000 øre (100,00 kr)
vatAmount  = 10000 × 0.25 / 1.25    =  2000 øre

periodStart is the time of the last charge. Read it from the newest item in GET /subscriptions/{id}/charges, or from lastChargeAt on the subscription.

Prorate only an active subscription with a valid paid period: lastChargeAt and nextChargeAt are both set, and lastChargeAt < now < nextChargeAt. A trialing subscription has lastChargeAt: null. The customer has paid nothing yet, so there is no difference to charge. For a trial, PATCH the new price only.

  1. Read the subscription and its last chargeGet status, amount, nextChargeAt and lastChargeAt.
  2. Check the periodContinue only if the subscription is active and lastChargeAt < now < nextChargeAt. For a trial, skip to the PATCH step.
  3. Calculate the differenceUse the formula. If the difference is below your minimum, skip the charge.
  4. Record the upgrade before you chargeStore an upgrade record with the state charging. If your process stops, this record tells you where to resume.
  5. Charge the differenceCall POST /subscriptions/{id}/charge with amount, vatRate, vatAmount and a reason such as "upgrade_proration".
  6. Check resultCodestatus: "charge_created" only says Plorea accepted the request. resultCode: "Authorised" says the bank approved. Verified
  7. Save the charge at onceStore chargeId and reference on the upgrade record. Set the state to charged. Do this before the PATCH.
  8. PATCH the new priceSend the new amount, vatAmount and, if you use it, quantity and title. Then set the state to done.
  9. Resume, do not repeatIf the PATCH fails, retry the PATCH only. An upgrade in the state charged is already paid. If the charge request timed out, read /charges before you charge again.

use MemberFlow\Plorea\Data\Amount;
use MemberFlow\Plorea\Exceptions\ChargeFailedException;
use MemberFlow\Plorea\Exceptions\ValidationException;
use MemberFlow\Plorea\Facades\Plorea;

$subscriptions = Plorea::subscriptions();
$current = $subscriptions->find('sub_test_acme_001');

// $upgrade is your own record of this upgrade (for example an Eloquent model).
// In the state 'charged' the difference is already paid: skip to the PATCH.
if ($upgrade->state !== 'charged') {
    $periodStart = $current->lastChargeAt;
    $periodEnd = $current->nextChargeAt;
    $now = now();

    // Prorate only an active subscription with a valid paid period.
    // A trial has lastChargeAt null: change the price only.
    $hasPaidPeriod = $current->isActive()
        && $periodStart !== null && $periodEnd !== null
        && $periodStart->lessThan($now) && $now->lessThan($periodEnd);

    if ($hasPaidPeriod) {
        $ratio = $now->diffInSeconds($periodEnd) / $periodStart->diffInSeconds($periodEnd);
        $difference = (int) round((39900 - $current->amount->value) * $ratio);
        $vat = (int) round($difference * 0.25 / 1.25);

        $upgrade->update(['state' => 'charging']);

        try {
            $charge = $subscriptions->charge(
                $current->id,
                amount: Amount::nok($difference),
                reason: 'upgrade_proration',
                vatRate: 0.25,
                vatAmount: $vat,
            );
        } catch (ChargeFailedException $e) {
            // 402: the card was declined. Keep the old plan.
            return;
        } catch (ValidationException $e) {
            // 400: not chargeable (canceled, or card not active).
            return;
        }

        if ($charge->resultCode !== 'Authorised') {
            return;
        }

        // Save the charge before the PATCH, so a PATCH failure never repeats it.
        $upgrade->update([
            'state' => 'charged',
            'charge_id' => $charge->id,
            'charge_reference' => $charge->reference,
        ]);
    }
}

// Throws on failure. Retry later: the state stays 'charged', so no new charge.
$subscriptions->update($current->id)
    ->title('Acme Gym premium')
    ->amount(Amount::nok(39900))
    ->vat(rate: 0.25, amount: 7980)
    ->save();

$upgrade->update(['state' => 'done']);

Pitfalls

  • Read nextChargeAt in the charge response. The response includes it. Whether a manual charge moves the schedule of later renewals is not confirmed. Compare nextChargeAt before and after the charge. Open question
  • A manual charge emits payment.authorised, not subscription.charge_succeeded. Do not treat it as a renewal in your webhook handler. Verified
  • A manual charge reference resolves on GET /payments/status/{reference}. A scheduler charge reference does not. Verified

Downgrade

Use this when a customer moves to a cheaper plan. The simple and safe pattern is "downgrade at period end": the customer keeps the current plan until nextChargeAt, and the next renewal uses the lower price.

  1. Record the pending changeStore the new plan and the date it takes effect (nextChargeAt) in your database.
  2. PATCH the lower price nowSend amount, vatAmount and quantity. The PATCH does not charge or refund. The current period is already paid. Verified
  3. Switch features at period endKeep the old features until nextChargeAt. Switch when subscription.charge_succeeded arrives with the lower amount.

An immediate downgrade with a partial refund is not supported yet

To refund part of the current period you need to refund a scheduler charge. A scheduler charge reference does not resolve on GET /payments/status/{reference} (403, later 404). It is not known whether POST /payments/refund accepts it. Open question

Until Plorea confirms, give the difference as credit in your own system, or downgrade at period end.

Pause or freeze

Use this when a customer stops paying for a known period and then continues, for example a gym membership frozen during an injury or a summer break. The customer keeps the card on file. No charge happens during the pause.

Native pause, with one open question

Plorea recommends PATCH /subscriptions/{id} with {"status": "paused"} to pause, and {"status": "active", "nextChargeAt": "…"} to resume. Stated by Plorea Both calls work as described in test. Verified In test, no charge was seen after a resume. Plorea has the question. Open question Production is unobserved.

Fallback: cancel, then a new subscription with a trial

Use this only while the open question above blocks you, or in an environment where the resume charge has not been proven. The charge at the end of a trial is verified. The cost is a new subscription ID and a cancel that cannot be undone safely.

  1. Prepare the new subscription firstRead the old subscription. Build the full create request before you cancel: card, amount, interval, VAT, externalId and the company. A read returns merchantOrgNr, but not merchantName or merchantEmail. Verified Take the name and email from your own record. Send optional fields such as customerId only when they have a value.
  2. Cancel the current subscriptionCall POST /subscriptions/{id}/cancel with reason: "paused". Check that status is canceled. If not, stop. Do not create the new subscription.
  3. Create the new subscription on the same cardSet trialEndsAt to the resume date, or to accessEndsAt if that is later. Use the same externalId.
  4. Block access during the pauseThe new subscription reports trialing. Use your own pause record to know that this trial is a pause.

Pitfalls

  • A pause does not end on its own. If your resume job does not run, billing never restarts. Monitor the job.
  • Webhooks do not tell you about the pause. Plorea's catalogue has no event for pause, resume, cancel or reactivate. Stated by Plorea
  • A resume with a lapsed date looks overdue. A resumed subscription that was not charged stays active with a nextChargeAt in the past. Your overdue check flags it after the grace period. That is the open question above.
  • Check the card before the resume date. A card that expires during the pause fails on the first charge. Read expiryDate on GET /payment-methods/{id} and ask for a new card early.

Change the billing interval

Use this when a customer moves from monthly to yearly billing, or the other way. PATCH cannot change interval. Verified You end the old subscription and start a new one that begins billing when the paid period ends.

  1. Read the old subscription firstNote trialEndsAt. The cancel response does not return it. A read returns merchantOrgNr, but not the merchant name or email. Verified Take those two from your own record.
  2. Cancel the old subscriptionCheck that the call succeeded and that status is canceled. If it failed, stop. Do not create the new subscription. Read accessEndsAt from the response.
  3. Choose the start dateUse accessEndsAt. If it is null, the old subscription was in its trial. Verified Use the old trialEndsAt instead. Check that the start date is in the future. Without a future trial date, the new subscription bills at once. Verified
  4. Create the new subscriptionUse the same paymentMethodId and company. Set the new interval and amount. Set trialEndsAt to the start date.
  5. Link the two subscriptionsKeep the same externalId. Store the old ID in metadata.

Replace the card

Use this when the card expires, is lost, or a renewal fails. The subscription keeps its ID, price and schedule. Only the stored payment method changes.

  1. Start a new card setupCall POST /payment-methods/setup with the same shopperReference, tenantId and customerId, and recurringType: "Subscription".
  2. Wait for activePoll GET /payment-methods/{id}. Do not PATCH a pending_setup or failed method.
  3. PATCH the subscriptionSend the new paymentMethodId. A method that is not active returns 400 "Payment method is not active". Verified
  4. Collect a missed renewalIf a renewal failed before the swap, check Failed charges and dunning for when to charge now.

Never retry a charge blindly

The charge endpoint has no idempotency key. After a timeout or a 5xx, read GET /subscriptions/{id}/charges and look for your charge before you send it again. Verified
