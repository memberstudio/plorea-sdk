Refunds and cancellations

A cancel stops money that has not moved yet. A refund sends back money that has moved. Both are modifications of an existing payment. Both are asynchronous: Plorea accepts the request at once, and the provider settles it later, sometimes many hours later.

Cancel or refund?

An authorisation reserves the money on the customer's card. A capture moves it. With Plorea, capture is automatic: Plorea settles through AmendoPOS a few minutes after the authorisation. There is no capture endpoint and no field that tells you whether the capture has happened. Stated by Plorea

This gives a short cancel window and a simple rule:

Use whenThe payment is authorised and not yet settled. In practice: minutes after the authorisation.The payment has settled. In practice: everything older than a few minutes.

What a late cancel does is unknown

A cancel sent nine days after the authorisation was accepted and reads cancel_requested. Verified Whether the provider then turns it into a refund, or rejects it, has not been observed. Open question Until Plorea documents this, prefer a refund for any payment that is not brand new.

Send X-Environment, or you hit live

A refund request without the X-Environment header goes to Plorea's live Adyen credentials. With a test key, the answer is 401 with "errorType": "security". Nothing in the answer says "wrong environment", so it looks like a bad API key. Watch for this when you copy a request into a terminal. Verified

  -H "Authorization: Bearer $PLOREA_API_KEY" \
  -H "X-Environment: test" \

Partial refunds

Send an amount lower than the payment to refund part of it. The request accepts the field, and the response echoes it. A partial refund has not been taken through to settlement in test. Open question Two more things are not documented:

  • Whether a second partial refund on the same payment is accepted, and what the status shows between the two.
  • What the top-level status is after a partial refund settles. refunded would hide the part that is still paid.

The status body keeps only the last refund (lastRefund*). Keep your own ledger of every refund you send, keyed on modificationReference.

Track the modification

The payment's own status changes immediately to refund_requested or cancel_requested. It stays there until the provider settles. There is no separate status per refund. Verified

These describe Plorea's inbound Adyen notification for the original authorisation. They stay AUTHORISATION / true during a refund. They do not track the refund.

How long settlement takes

Plan in hours, not minutes

One test refund took more than 11 hours to settle. Verified A refund and a cancel requested on the same day were both still in the _requested state more than 24 hours later. Plorea says this is expected. Stated by Plorea Whether live is faster is not known. Open question

So:

  • Book on the request. When the API answers refund_requested, record the refund in your books and tell the customer it is on its way.
  • Never time out into "failed". A job that polls every few minutes and gives up after one hour will report a healthy refund as failed. Do not reverse a refund in your books because time passed.
  • Use the webhook, poll as a backstop. Listen for payment.refunded. Poll the status a few times a day as a backstop, because a lost webhook is not redelivered. See Webhooks.
  • Subscription cancels are poll-only. Plorea sends no webhook for a subscription cancel. Stated by Plorea

Retry safely

Retry safety for refund and cancel is not known. Open question The modificationReference is your reference for one attempt. Plorea has not confirmed that it deduplicates a repeated reference. Treat it as a reference, not as an idempotency key. Do not retry a refund or a cancel automatically.

  1. Create the modification reference before the call.Derive it from your own refund record, for example {reference}-refund-{n}. Save it first.
  2. Send the request.On a timeout or a 5xx, the outcome is unknown, not failed.
  3. Read the payment status.If lastRefundReference (or lastCancelReference) equals your modification reference, Plorea accepted the request. Stop.
  4. Otherwise, escalate to a person.A missing match does not prove that Plorea rejected the request. The request can still be in flight, and a later refund overwrites the lastRefund* fields. A person decides whether to send it again, with the same modification reference.

Ask Plorea to confirm how it handles a repeated modification reference. Until then, the status check lowers the risk of a double refund. It does not remove the risk.

Refunds on subscription charges

A manual subscription charge has a reference of the form {subscriptionId}-{chargeId}. That reference resolves on GET /payments/status. Verified A refund on that reference has not been tested. It should work like a refund on any payment, but this is not confirmed. Open question A scheduled charge reference does not resolve on the status endpoint. Whether such a charge can be refunded through this API is not known. Open question See Subscription workflows.
